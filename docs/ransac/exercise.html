Exercise: RANSAC

Goal

By the end of this exercise you should be able to:

  • run a few RANSAC iterations by hand: fit a minimal sample, count inliers, choose,
  • compute the number of iterations for a given inlier fraction and sample size,
  • choose \varepsilon and predict what goes wrong when it is too small or too large,
  • decide between voting (Hough) and hypothesise-and-verify (RANSAC) for a problem,
  • (math) derive the iteration formula and the least-squares refits for lines and circles.

Do the pen-and-paper part first, then the demo.

Background

Idea What it means
Minimal sample the fewest points that determine the model: s = 2 (line), 3 (circle), 3 pairs (affine transform)
Inliers points within distance \varepsilon of the model
RANSAC repeat N times: sample, fit exactly, count inliers; keep the best; refit on its inliers
Success probability P = 1 - (1 - w^s)^N, w = inlier fraction
Iterations N = \log(1 - p)/\log(1 - w^s), rounded up
Line distance |x\cos\theta + y\sin\theta - \rho| for a line in normal form

By hand

B1. Eight points: (0, 0), (1, 1), (2, 2), (3, 3), (4, 4.2), (5, 1), (1, 4), (4, 0). Use \varepsilon = 0.5. (a) Sample 1 is \{(0, 0), (3, 3)\}. Give the line through them in normal form and the distance of every point to it. How many inliers? (b) Sample 2 is \{(5, 1), (1, 4)\}. Show that the line through them is 3x + 4y = 19, i.e. normal (0.6, 0.8) and \rho = 3.8. Distances and inliers? (c) Which model does RANSAC keep? Which points are used in the refit, and roughly where will the refitted line be?

B2. Iterations for a 99 % chance of at least one all-inlier sample: (a) a line with w = 0.5; (b) a circle with w = 0.5; (c) a line with w = 0.2; (d) a circle with w = 0.3. (e) With w = 0.2 and only the 17 iterations of (a), what is the chance of success?

B3. Voting or guessing? Explain your choice. (a) A single straight filament in an image where 90 % of the edge points are clutter. (b) Fitting an affine transform (6 parameters, 3 point pairs per sample) to matched points of two images, with 40 % wrong matches. (c) Finding all 20 straight edges of a building facade.

B4. The inliers of a line scatter with a standard deviation of 0.8 px. (a) Suggest \varepsilon. (b) A second line runs parallel, 8 px away. What is the largest \varepsilon that keeps the two lines apart, and what happens above it?

Solutions

B1. (a) The line y = x: normal (\cos 135°, \sin 135°) = (-0.707, 0.707), \rho = 0 (any sign convention: distance |x - y|/\sqrt2). Distances: 0, 0, 0, 0, 0.14, 2.83, 2.12, 2.83. 5 inliers: the first five points. (b) The direction from (5, 1) to (1, 4) is (-4, 3), so the normal is (3, 4)/5 = (0.6, 0.8); \rho = 0.6 \cdot 5 + 0.8 \cdot 1 = 3.8, i.e. 3x + 4y = 19. Distances |3x + 4y - 19|/5: 3.8, 2.4, 1.0, 0.4, 1.96, 0, 0, 1.4. 3 inliers: (3, 3), (5, 1), (1, 4). (c) Sample 1 (5 inliers). The refit uses (0, 0), (1, 1), (2, 2), (3, 3), (4, 4.2): the total-least-squares line through their centroid (2, 2.04), at about 46.1° (slightly steeper than 45°, pulled by (4, 4.2)). The three outliers play no part.

B2. (a) \log 0.01/\log(1 - 0.25) = 16.0: 17. (b) \log 0.01/\log(1 - 0.125) = 34.5: 35. (c) \log 0.01/\log(1 - 0.04) = 112.8: 113. (d) w^3 = 0.027: \log 0.01/\log 0.973 = 168.2: 169. (e) 1 - 0.96^{17} = 0.50: a coin toss.

B3. (a) Hough. RANSAC with w = 0.1, s = 2 needs \log 0.01/\log 0.99 = 459 samples; the Hough transform with gradient voting handles a 90 % clutter fraction with a fixed amount of work, as long as the filament’s peak stands above the floor. (b) RANSAC: w = 0.6, s = 3: \log 0.01/\log(1 - 0.216) = 19 samples; a 6D accumulator with 20 steps per parameter has 20^6 = 64 million cells. (c) Hough: 20 instances of a 2-parameter model, all found in one pass; RANSAC would need 20 sequential runs, the first ones with a small inlier fraction (w \approx 1/20 per line among the edge points).

B4. (a) 2 to 3 times the noise: \varepsilon \approx 2 px (the demo uses 2). (b) A model halfway between the lines is 4 px from each; with \varepsilon \ge 4 its band contains both lines, so it beats each true line and the two merge. Keep \varepsilon well below 4 (2 is fine).

Math questions

B5. (math) Derive N = \log(1 - p)/\log(1 - w^s). Show that for small w^s, N \approx 4.6/w^s when p = 0.99. By what factor does N grow when w halves, for a line and for a circle?

B6. (math) Show that the total-least-squares line (minimising the sum of squared perpendicular distances) passes through the centroid and that its normal is the eigenvector of the scatter matrix S with the smaller eigenvalue. Check with B1(c): the five inliers have centroid (2, 2.04); is the refitted line close to y = x?

B7. (math) Write the algebraic circle fit as a linear least-squares problem in (a, b, c) with c = r^2 - a^2 - b^2. Set up the system for the four points (5, 0), (0, 5), (-5, 0), (0, -5) and solve it. What quantity does the algebraic fit minimise, and why is it not exactly the geometric distance?

Solutions (math)

B5. A sample is all-inlier with probability w^s; samples are independent, so all N fail with probability (1 - w^s)^N. Requiring (1 - w^s)^N \le 1 - p and taking logarithms (both negative, so the inequality flips when dividing): N \ge \log(1 - p)/\log(1 - w^s). For small q = w^s, \log(1 - q) \approx -q, so N \approx -\log(0.01)/q = 4.6/w^s. Halving w multiplies w^s by 2^{-s}, so N grows about 4 times for a line and 8 times for a circle.

B6. \partial/\partial\rho \sum_i(\mathbf n^\top\mathbf p_i - \rho)^2 = -2\sum_i(\mathbf n^\top\mathbf p_i - \rho) = 0 gives \rho = \mathbf n^\top\bar{\mathbf p}. The remaining cost is \mathbf n^\top S\,\mathbf n with S = \sum_i(\mathbf p_i - \bar{\mathbf p})(\mathbf p_i - \bar{\mathbf p})^\top; over unit vectors its minimum is the smallest eigenvalue, attained at its eigenvector (Rayleigh quotient). For B1(c): the deviations from the centroid are (-2, -2.04), (-1, -1.04), (0, -0.04), (1, 0.96), (2, 2.16); S has a dominant eigenvector at 46.1° and the normal at 136.1°: close to y = x, tilted by 1.1° towards (4, 4.2).

B7. (x - a)^2 + (y - b)^2 = r^2 \iff x^2 + y^2 = 2ax + 2by + c. Each point gives one row: [2x_i \;\; 2y_i \;\; 1]\,(a, b, c)^\top = x_i^2 + y_i^2. For the four points: rows (10, 0, 1), (0, 10, 1), (-10, 0, 1), (0, -10, 1), right-hand side 25 each. The normal equations A^\top A = \operatorname{diag}(200, 200, 4), A^\top \mathbf z = (0, 0, 100) give a = b = 0, c = 25, r = \sqrt{25} = 5. The fit minimises \sum_i (x_i^2 + y_i^2 - 2ax_i - 2by_i - c)^2 = \sum_i (d_i^2 - r^2)^2 = \sum_i (d_i - r)^2(d_i + r)^2, with d_i the distance of point i from the centre: the geometric error (d_i - r) weighted by (d_i + r)^2, so points outside the circle count more than points inside. For exact or nearly exact data it agrees with the geometric fit; it is linear and needs no starting guess.

Solutions

R1. Least squares: \theta = 68.6°, \rho = 59.1, visibly tilted by the outliers. RANSAC (refit): \theta = 62.9°, \rho = 58.0, with 41 inliers, all of them true inliers (42 points are on the line).

R2. Samples 2, 3 and 14 are all-inlier, with 29, 29 and 30 inliers; the best is sample 14 (30). Mixed samples get 3 to 16. The refit uses 31 inliers (29 true, 2 outliers that happen to lie within 2 px): \theta = 63.4°, \rho = 58.1, against \theta = 75.8°, \rho = 56.4 for least squares.

R3. In all ten runs at least one sample is all-inlier, and the refit lands within about 1° of 63° and 0.3 of 58 (for example seed 4: the very first sample is all-inlier). That fits 99 %: a failure is expected in about 1 run in 100.

R4. 50 % (1 - 0.96^{17}). With seed 1 no sample is all-inlier; the best is a chance alignment of 10 points (4 true inliers) at \theta = 85.9°, \rho = 53.2: wrong. The Hough peak is at \theta = 63°, \rho = 58.2 with 22 votes: right. With 113 iterations the best model appears at iteration 18 (22 inliers), and the refit is \theta = 62.1°, \rho = 58.7 with 24 inliers (18 true). The all-inlier samples (iterations 91 and 93) get only 8 and 7 inliers: their two points are close together, so the noise of 0.8 px tilts the line through them a lot. A sample with a long baseline (even one containing an outlier that happens to lie near the line, as at iteration 18) fits better.

R5. The refit is \theta = 74.1°, \rho = 54.6, with 42 inliers: halfway between the two lines (\rho = 50 and 58), with both inside its band. With \varepsilon = 2: \theta = 76.4°, \rho = 50.9, 24 inliers, all true: one of the two lines.

R6. Least squares: \theta = 163.1°, \rho = -28.9, a line that fits neither. First model: \theta = 131.8°, \rho = -10.2 (21 inliers); second, after removing those: \theta = 71.4°, \rho = 62.2 (21 inliers). Both match the true lines.

R7. 35 iterations (s = 3, w = 0.5). RANSAC refit: centre (51.8, 48.4), r = 27.8. Least squares on all points: centre (51.5, 48.2) but r = 34.8. The outliers are spread evenly around the circle, so they barely move the centre, but on average they lie farther out than the circle, so the radius grows.

R8. With \varepsilon = 0.5 (smaller than the noise of 0.8) most true inliers fall outside the band: the best model has only 10 inliers (9 true), and the refit \theta = 64.4°, \rho = 58.2 rests on few points. The counts are small and noisy, so the choice of the best model is close to chance. \varepsilon should be 2 to 3 times the noise.

Where this leads

RANSAC returns the best model and its inliers; with matched feature points between two images the same loop estimates the transform that aligns them, which is the subject of the planned Motion elective. The other days of the Detection group take a different route to finding objects: integral images make box filters so cheap that a detector can test millions of windows.
