# Plan: a day on colour spaces

Internal working doc (plan/ is not rendered). Status: draft for discussion, 2026-11-12.

## Goal of the day

After the day a student can:

1. explain why colour is **three numbers**: a spectrum is (almost) infinite-dimensional, and the eye or a camera applies a **linear map** to $\mathbb{R}^3$ (three cone types, or three filters); compute cone responses for a small sampled spectrum, and construct a **metamer** from the null space of that map;
2. read a **CIE xy chromaticity diagram**: the spectral locus, the white point, why mixtures lie on straight lines, and why a display's **gamut** is a triangle;
3. convert between **linear and gamma-encoded sRGB**, explain why blurring, resizing and averaging must be done in linear light, and build the **3×3 matrix** from a display's primaries and white point to CIE XYZ (and the matrices to YCbCr and luma);
4. compute **HSV** and **CIELAB** coordinates of a colour, explain where HSV is singular (grey) and why hue is an angle, use **ΔE** in Lab as a colour difference, and choose a space in which a colour **segmentation** becomes a simple threshold;
5. correct the colour of an image as a linear map: **white balance** (von Kries diagonal scaling, grey world, white patch) and **colour deconvolution** of histology stains (Beer–Lambert optical density and the inverse of a 3×3 stain matrix), and say when each fails.

Why it fits this audience: nearly every step is linear algebra they already own.
Colour vision is a rank-3 linear map with a large null space (metamers), a change of
primaries is a change of basis, white balance is a diagonal matrix in a well-chosen
basis, and stain separation is solving $\mathbf{od} = M^\top\mathbf{c}$. The non-linear
parts (gamma, HSV, Lab) are single-variable functions whose shape can be drawn. The
spectrum integrals $\int s(\lambda)\,\bar{x}(\lambda)\,d\lambda$ become sums the moment
the spectrum is sampled, which is how the demos and the by-hand exercises treat them.

## Units (one folder each: lec, quiz, exercise with #explore)

Four core units, about 1.5 h each including the exercise, plus one optional unit.

### 1. `colour-vision/`: From a spectrum to three numbers

- Motivation: two lamps that look the same white but make a red apple look different; a camera that sees two fabrics as different colours while we see them as equal. Colour is not a property of light alone.
- **Light is a spectrum** $s(\lambda)$, roughly 380–730 nm: picture of daylight, an incandescent bulb (Planck), a white LED (blue peak + phosphor hump), a laser line. Reflectance $r(\lambda)$ of a surface; what reaches the eye is $s(\lambda)\,r(\lambda)$.
- **Three cone types** L, M, S with broad overlapping sensitivities $\bar{l}, \bar{m}, \bar{s}$. Response $L = \int s(\lambda)\,\bar{l}(\lambda)\,d\lambda$, etc. Sample at 10 nm: $s \in \mathbb{R}^{36}$, and $\mathbf{c} = A\mathbf{s}$ with $A \in \mathbb{R}^{3 \times 36}$. **Colour vision is a linear map of rank 3.** A camera is the same with its own $A_{\text{cam}}$.
- **Metamers**: $A\mathbf{s}_1 = A\mathbf{s}_2$ whenever $\mathbf{s}_2 - \mathbf{s}_1 \in \ker A$, a 33-dimensional subspace. Constraint: $\mathbf{s}_2 \ge 0$ (a physical spectrum). Why displays work at all: three primaries can produce a metamer of (many) real spectra. Why they fail: an eye–camera mismatch ($\ker A \ne \ker A_{\text{cam}}$), and an illuminant change can break a metameric match (metameric failure; links to unit 4).
- Colour blindness in one slide: a dichromat has rank 2 (one cone type missing), so more pairs are metamers. (Simulation as a projection: optional unit.)
- **Colour matching** (Wright–Guild): a test light matched by three primaries; some matches need **negative** amounts (the primary added to the test side). The colour matching functions $\bar{r}, \bar{g}, \bar{b}$ are a linear combination of the cone fundamentals (another basis of the same 3D row space).
- **CIE XYZ** (1931): a change of basis chosen so that $\bar{x}, \bar{y}, \bar{z} \ge 0$ everywhere and $\bar{y}$ = luminous efficiency, so $Y$ = **luminance**. All real colours have $X, Y, Z \ge 0$; the XYZ "primaries" themselves are not physical.
- **Chromaticity**: $x = X/(X+Y+Z)$, $y = Y/(X+Y+Z)$: central projection onto the plane $X+Y+Z = 1$ (picture), forgetting brightness. The **spectral locus** (monochromatic lights) is the horseshoe; the purple line closes it. Additive mixtures lie on the **straight line** between the two chromaticities (the projection maps lines to lines), so a display with three primaries reaches exactly the **triangle** between them: its **gamut**. sRGB, Display P3, Rec. 2020 compared. The white point (D65) inside.
- Honest caveat on the projector: the slides cannot show colours outside the projector's gamut; the horseshoe is drawn with out-of-gamut colours clipped and marked.
- **Demo `spectrum`** (new, main demo of the unit): top, a spectrum on a 380–730 nm axis, edited by dragging ~12 control points (smooth interpolation) or chosen from presets (daylight D65, blackbody with temperature slider, white LED, three laser lines, fluorescent tube with spikes). Toggle *observer: eye / camera / dichromat*: the same pair is then no longer a match (two swatches split). Toggle *illuminant*: multiply both by a lamp spectrum (the pair as reflectances): the match breaks under the other lamp. Presets: "daylight vs LED: same white", "make a metamer", "the camera disagrees", "metameric failure under tungsten", "a dichromat sees more matches", "monochromatic light at 580 nm vs red + green".
- **Demo `chromaticity`** (new, small, SVG): the xy diagram with the spectral locus (wavelength ticks) and filled approximate colours. Gamut triangles of sRGB / Display P3 / Rec. 2020 (toggles). Drag two points: the mixture line and a slider for the mixing weight (with the weight in XYZ, not in xy: the point moves non-uniformly along the line, a nice surprise). *Custom display* mode: drag the three primaries and the white point; the RGB→XYZ matrix of unit 2 is shown live with its inverse. ### 2. `rgb-linear/`: RGB as a device space: gamma and linear maps

- **RGB is a device space**: "(255, 0, 0)" means "full drive of this display's red primary". Without primaries and a white point it is not a colour. **sRGB** (IEC 61966-2-1) fixes primaries (the unit 1 triangle), white D65 and a transfer function; it is what an untagged 8-bit image and the browser canvas assume.
- **Gamma encoding**: perceived lightness is roughly a power ~1/2.4–1/3 of luminance, so 8 bits are spent better on encoded values. sRGB transfer: $C_{\text{lin}} = C/12.92$ for $C \le 0.04045$, else $\big((C + 0.055)/1.055\big)^{2.4}$; inverse for encoding. Picture: the curve, and an 8-bit linear ramp vs encoded ramp (banding in the darks). Camera raw data and microscope detectors are **linear**; JPEG photographs are **encoded**.
- **Why it matters for image analysis**: blur, resampling, averaging, mixing and photometric measurement are linear operations on **light**. Done on encoded values they are wrong: the average of black (0) and white (255) is 128 in encoded values, which is only 21.6 % of white's luminance; the correct mid-grey of the light is linear 0.5, encoded **188**. Consequences: dark halos when blurring bright details, wrong colours where red meets green (a muddy dark band), images that darken when downsampled, wrong intensity ratios. In quantitative microscopy: never measure on gamma-encoded exports.
- **Linear maps between spaces**: in linear RGB, a colour is $\mathbf{c} = R\,\mathbf{p}_R + G\,\mathbf{p}_G + B\,\mathbf{p}_B$, so $\mathbf{XYZ} = M\,\mathbf{rgb}$ with the primaries' XYZ as columns. The columns are scaled so that $M\,(1,1,1)^\top$ = white point: given chromaticities of the primaries and the white, solve one $3 \times 3$ system for the three scales. sRGB → XYZ:
  $M = \begin{pmatrix} 0.4124 & 0.3576 & 0.1805 \\ 0.2126 & 0.7152 & 0.0722 \\ 0.0193 & 0.1192 & 0.9505 \end{pmatrix}$.
  The middle row is the **luminance** weights. RGB of display A → RGB of display B $= M_B^{-1} M_A$: a change of basis. Out-of-gamut colours give components $< 0$ or $> 1$; clipping vs gamut mapping in one slide.
- **Converting to greyscale** (picture: one photo, four greys): luminance $Y = 0.2126R + 0.7152G + 0.0722B$ in **linear** RGB; the video-standard **luma** $Y' = 0.299R' + 0.587G' + 0.114B'$ on encoded values (an approximation); the channel mean (treats the eye as equally sensitive to blue); a single channel (often right for fluorescence or stained tissue: pick the channel with the contrast). An isoluminant test image (red text on green of equal luminance) disappears under luminance but not under the mean: "greyscale" is a choice, not a fact.
- **Opponent spaces and YCbCr**: the visual system recodes cone signals into an achromatic and two opponent channels (red–green, blue–yellow). **YCbCr** (JPEG, full range): $Y' = 0.299R' + 0.587G' + 0.114B'$, $C_b = 128 + 0.564(B' - Y')$, $C_r = 128 + 0.713(R' - Y')$: a $3 \times 3$ matrix plus an offset. **Chroma subsampling** (4:2:0): store $C_b, C_r$ at half resolution in both directions; the image looks unchanged, while subsampling $Y'$ the same way is obvious. Recall from `fourier/`: the eye's colour-difference channels are low-pass.
- **Demo `gammablur`** (new): an image and a Gaussian blur / downsample, computed twice: on encoded values (naive) and in linear light (decode → blur → encode). Side by side with a difference image and a draggable 1D profile showing both curves. Controls: $\sigma$, *blur / downsample ×2, ×4 / mix two colours*, a toggle to show linear values directly (looks too dark: why we encode). Inputs: thin white lines on black, a red/green checker, "lights at night" (synthetic bright points on dark, seeded), a colour photo (see open questions), a fluorescence-like image. Presets: "black + white = 128 or 188?" (with a fine stripe pattern next to two uniform patches, to compare by squinting, with a caveat that the projector may not follow sRGB), "red meets green: the dark seam", "blurred lights lose their glow", "downsampling darkens fine detail".
- **Demo `colourmatrix`** (new): a colour image, a $3 \times 3$ matrix (editable cells, with offset) and the transformed channels shown as three grey images plus the recombined colour image. Modes (`data-mode`): *sRGB→XYZ*, *YCbCr* (with chroma subsampling factor 1/2/4/8 for $C_bC_r$ or for $Y'$), *greyscale* (weights sliders constrained to sum to 1, presets luminance / luma / mean / single channel, applied to linear or encoded values), *display A→B* (from the `chromaticity` custom display). Presets: "Y′CbCr channels", "subsample chroma by 4: invisible", "subsample luma by 4: obvious", "the isoluminant test", "greyscale of a stained tissue: the best single channel".
- By hand: (a) decode 0, 128, 188, 255 with the sRGB transfer function; the average of black and white in encoded and in linear values; (b) build the RGB→XYZ matrix of a toy display from given XYZ of its primaries (unscaled) and a white point: solve for the three scales; check the white row sum; (c) $Y'C_bC_r$ of pure red and of mid-grey; (d) count the bytes saved by 4:2:0.

### 3. `colour-perceptual/`: HSV, CIELAB, colour differences and colour segmentation

- The question for analysis: "find the red objects", "how different are these two colours?" Neither is easy in RGB: a shadow changes all three channels, and equal RGB distances are not equal perceived differences.
- **HSV / HSL** (Smith 1978): a re-parametrisation of the RGB cube for picking colours. $V = \max(R,G,B)$, $S = (\max - \min)/\max$, $H$ = angle around the grey diagonal, computed piecewise on a hexagon (one slide, picture of the cube seen along its diagonal). Properties:
  - **Hue is an angle**: red sits at both 0° and 360°, so a hue threshold for red is a union of two intervals, and the mean hue needs the circular mean (average the unit vectors $(\cos H, \sin H)$).
  - **Singular at grey**: for $\max = \min$ the hue is undefined and $S$ is 0/0 at black. Near grey, a tiny noise flips the hue arbitrarily: a hue mask on a noisy image speckles in dark and grey regions; fix with a saturation (and value) floor.
  - Hue and saturation are (nearly) invariant to **shading** (scaling all channels): why a hue threshold survives shadows where an R-channel threshold fails. Same idea: **normalised rgb** $r = R/(R+G+B)$, $g = G/(R+G+B)$, the camera's own chromaticity.
  - HSV is not perceptual and not device-independent: "V" of pure blue and pure yellow are both 1.
- **CIELAB** (1976): $L^* = 116 f(Y/Y_n) - 16$, $a^* = 500\,[f(X/X_n) - f(Y/Y_n)]$, $b^* = 200\,[f(Y/Y_n) - f(Z/Z_n)]$, with $f(t) = t^{1/3}$ above a small threshold and linear below. Read: lightness from a cube root (like gamma), and two **opponent** axes (green–red, blue–yellow) as differences of compressed responses; the white point $(X_n, Y_n, Z_n)$ is built in (a crude white balance). Polar form: chroma $C^*_{ab}$, hue angle $h_{ab}$. CIELUV in one line (similar goal, a chromaticity diagram that is more uniform).
- **ΔE**: $\Delta E^*_{ab}$ = Euclidean distance in Lab; ≈ 1–2.3 is a just-noticeable difference. Lab is only approximately uniform (MacAdam ellipses picture); CIEDE2000 corrects it (formula not taught, used in the demo as a toggle). Contrast with Euclidean distance in sRGB: two pairs with the same RGB distance, one nearly invisible (dark blues), one obvious (greens).
- **Choosing a space for segmentation**: the pixels of an image are a **point cloud in 3D**; a segmentation is a region of that space. A good space makes the region simple: a hue wedge with a saturation floor (HSV), a disc or box in $a^*b^*$, a ball of radius $\Delta E$ around a reference colour (Lab), or two/three clusters (k-means in Lab: isodata from the thresholding day in 3D). A 2D histogram over the two chromatic axes ($H$–$S$, $a^*$–$b^*$, $C_b$–$C_r$, $r$–$g$) is the picture in which to draw the region; Otsu on a single derived channel (hue, $a^*$, or a stain channel from unit 4) is the one-threshold version (back-reference, `lib/threshold.ts`).
- Pointer: a per-class colour model (a Gaussian in Lab) is the data term of the graph-cut day (GrabCut).
- **Demo `colourcloud`** (new, main demo of the day, WebGL via `lib/gl3d.ts`): left, a colour image (photo, a synthetic scene of coloured objects with shading and noise, an H&E image); right, its pixels (subsampled to ~20–50k, seeded) as a rotatable 3D point cloud, each point drawn in its own colour, inside the outline of the sRGB gamut in the current space. Spaces: encoded sRGB (cube), linear RGB, XYZ, YCbCr, HSV (cone/cylinder, hue as angle), Lab. *Animate* morphs the cloud continuously from one space to another (interpolated positions), so students see the cube bend into a cone or into the Lab solid. Brushing both ways: paint on the image → the points light up; drag a box / ball / hue wedge in 3D → the pixels light up in the image. Picked pixel: its coordinates in all spaces. Toggles: show the grey axis, show the 2D projection onto the chromatic plane, noise level, shading (multiply the image by a gradient: the cloud stretches along rays through black). Presets: "the RGB cube becomes the HSV cone", "shadows are rays through black", "grey pixels scatter in hue", "Lab: the white point at the top", "an H&E slide: two stains, a 2D sheet". Also used in the optional unit (`data-mode="pca"`).
- **Demo `colourseg`** (new): image (left), 2D histogram of the chosen chromatic pair (middle, log scale), mask (right). Methods: per-channel RGB thresholds, hue interval + saturation and value floors (drawn as a wedge on the $H$–$S$ disc; the interval may wrap through 0°), ΔE ball around a clicked reference colour (radius slider, ΔE76 / ΔE2000, or Euclidean in sRGB for comparison), Otsu on a chosen channel, k-means with $k = 2..5$ in RGB or Lab (seeded initialisation, *step* button). Inputs: synthetic scene with shadows and noise (known ground truth, Dice readout via the thresholding day's evaluation code), colour photo, H&E. Presets: "R-channel threshold fails in the shadow", "hue survives the shadow", "red wraps around 0°", "grey and dark pixels speckle: add a saturation floor", "ΔE ball in Lab vs RGB distance", "k-means in RGB vs in Lab".

### 4. `colour-unmixing/`: White balance and colour deconvolution

- Common theme: find a $3 \times 3$ matrix (diagonal, or full) in the right space that turns measured colours into quantities with a meaning.
- **Colour constancy**: the camera sees $\mathbf{c} = A_{\text{cam}}\,\operatorname{diag}(\mathbf{s})\,\mathbf{r}$ (illuminant × reflectance). We want the colour under a reference light (D65). The brain does this; a camera must estimate the illuminant.
- **von Kries** (1902): scale each cone response independently, $\mathbf{c}' = D\,\mathbf{c}$ with $D = \operatorname{diag}(w_\text{ref}/w)$ for the white $w$. Exact when the sensor curves are narrow, approximate otherwise; better in a sharpened cone basis (Bradford: $M^{-1} D M$, a diagonal matrix in another basis, one slide). Must be applied to **linear** values (unit 2).
- Estimating the white: **grey world** (the average reflectance is grey: scale each channel to the same mean), **white patch / max-RGB** (the brightest pixel is white), a **clicked grey card**. Failure modes: a scene dominated by one colour (grey world turns a forest grey-magenta), a saturated highlight or clipped channel (white patch).
- In microscopy: brightfield **flat-field** and white reference (divide by the empty-field image per channel) is exactly a per-pixel white balance; it also fixes vignetting.
- **Colour deconvolution** (Ruifrok & Johnston 2001): brightfield stains **absorb**. Beer–Lambert per channel: $I_c = I_{0,c}\,10^{-\sum_k a_{kc} C_k}$ for stain concentrations $C_k$. Take **optical density** $\mathrm{OD}_c = -\log_{10}(I_c / I_{0,c})$: then $\mathbf{od} = \sum_k C_k\,\mathbf{m}_k = M^\top \mathbf{C}$, **linear** in the concentrations. Picture: in RGB the pixels of a two-stain slide lie on a curved surface; in OD space they fill a flat wedge spanned by the two stain vectors (`colourcloud` with an OD option).
  - Stain vectors $\mathbf{m}_k$ (unit rows of $M$), e.g. Ruifrok's hematoxylin ≈ (0.65, 0.70, 0.29), eosin ≈ (0.07, 0.99, 0.11), DAB ≈ (0.27, 0.57, 0.78).
  - $\mathbf{C} = (M^\top)^{-1}\,\mathbf{od}$ per pixel: three concentration images (H, E, residual; or H, DAB, residual). With two stains, complete $M$ with the cross product as a third ("residual") vector.
  - What goes wrong: wrong stain vectors → **crosstalk** (eosin appears in the H channel); nearly parallel stains → ill-conditioned $M$, noise amplified (condition number); dark saturated pixels ($I = 0$, log undefined, clip); wrong $I_0$; stains that **scatter** rather than absorb, or co-localise, break the linear model. DAB is not stoichiometric: its "concentration" is a relative measure (one slide of honesty for quantification).
  - Estimating stain vectors from the image: Macenko et al. (2009), the plane of the OD cloud by PCA and two extreme angles in it (pointer, one slide; link to the PCA days). Stain normalisation between scanners as one bullet.
  - Same linear model elsewhere: **spectral unmixing** of overlapping fluorophores (emission spectra as the columns, more channels than dyes → least squares). One slide; links back to unit 1.
- **Demo `whitebalance`** (new): a synthetic scene rendered **spectrally** (seeded smooth reflectance spectra for a 4×6 chart of patches + a few objects, times an illuminant spectrum, times a camera or the CIE observer), so the ground truth under D65 is known. Illuminant: blackbody 2500–10000 K slider, fluorescent with spikes, narrow-band RGB LED. Correction: none, grey world, white patch, click a patch, and the space it is applied in (encoded sRGB / linear RGB / LMS von Kries / Bradford), plus a full 3×3 least-squares fit to the chart. Readout: mean and max ΔE over the patches against the D65 rendering, per-patch ΔE as colour. Scene option *mostly green* and *with a specular highlight*. Presets: "tungsten 2800 K", "grey world works", "grey world in a forest", "white patch fooled by a highlight", "diagonal on encoded values", "narrow-band LED: no diagonal fixes it" (metameric failure from unit 1).
- **Demo `stainsep`** (new): a synthetic brightfield image generated by Beer–Lambert from known concentration maps (seeded: nuclei with hematoxylin, cytoplasm and stroma with eosin; mode H-DAB: a subset of nuclei DAB-positive), with detector noise and an $I_0$ slider; plus a real H&E / H-DAB image when licensed (see open questions). Panels: RGB image; OD point cloud with the stain vectors as arrows (2D projection, or 3D via `colourcloud`); the three concentration images (grey or pseudocoloured); for the synthetic image, the true maps and a crosstalk readout (correlation between recovered and true maps). The stain matrix is **editable**: drag each stain vector's tip on the OD unit sphere (shown as a 2D chromaticity-like triangle) or type entries; $M$, $(M^\top)^{-1}$ and the condition number shown live. Presets: "Ruifrok defaults", "the true vectors: perfect separation", "wrong eosin vector: crosstalk", "two stains, residual from the cross product", "nearly parallel stains: noise explodes", "wrong I₀ / saturated pixels", "estimate from the image (Macenko)", "H-DAB: count positive nuclei by thresholding the DAB channel" (Otsu, back-reference).
- By hand: (a) grey-world scales for given channel means, and the corrected value of one pixel; (b) a pixel with $I = (80, 120, 200)$, $I_0 = 255$: its OD; (c) with a given two-stain matrix restricted to two channels ($2 \times 2$), solve for the two concentrations; verify that mixing two stains is additive in OD and multiplicative in RGB; (d) compute the third (residual) vector as a cross product and the inverse of a small $3 \times 3$ $M$ (or check a given inverse); (e) the condition number of two given stain matrices, and which separation is noisier.

### 5. (optional / further reading) `colour-display/`: Pseudocolour, colour maps and decorrelation

- **Fluorescence** channels are grey images (one per filter set), not RGB: displaying them is a choice. **Pseudocolour / LUTs**: map intensity to a colour ramp per channel, merge additively. Red/green merges are invisible to ~8 % of men: use magenta/green or cyan/magenta/yellow; always show channels separately too. Display range (min/max) is part of the LUT; never let it silently clip data used for measurement.
- **Colour maps for scalar data**: lightness must be monotonic for sequential data (viridis, cividis), a diverging map for signed data (zero = neutral), a qualitative palette for labels. Jet/rainbow: non-monotonic $L^*$ creates false edges and hides real ones, and fails for colour-blind viewers. Picture: $L^*$ along each map; the same height field with jet vs viridis.
- **Simulating colour blindness** as a linear map: in LMS, a dichromat's response is a projection onto a plane (Brettel/Viénot/Machado); one $3 \times 3$ matrix of rank 2 in linear RGB. Use it to check figures.
- **Decorrelation stretch** (PCA on colour pixels): RGB of natural images is strongly correlated (the cloud is a cigar along the grey axis). Rotate to the principal axes (eigenvectors of the $3 \times 3$ covariance), equalise the variances, rotate back: faint colour differences become visible (remote sensing, faded stains, rock art). The first principal component as a data-driven greyscale.
- **Demo `colourmap`** (new, small): a grey image (fluorescence-like, a height field, a signed difference image), LUT menu (grey, viridis, cividis, magma, jet, a diverging map, a qualitative palette) with its $L^*$ profile plotted under the colour bar; display range sliders; *simulate deuteranopia / protanopia / tritanopia* toggle applied to the whole demo; a two- or three-channel fluorescence merge mode (red/green vs magenta/green). Presets: "jet invents edges", "viridis is monotone", "red/green merge for a deuteranope", "signed data needs a diverging map".
- **Demo**: reuse `colourcloud` with `data-mode="pca"`: the covariance ellipsoid and principal axes drawn in the cloud, a *decorrelation stretch* slider that morphs the cloud to the stretched one and updates the image.

## Shared engineering

- `demos/src/lib/colour.ts` (new; the home for everything colour, already suggested by the level-sets plan for colour maps): sRGB transfer function and inverse (also as 256-entry lookup tables), RGB↔XYZ matrices from primaries + white point, XYZ↔xy, XYZ↔Lab/LCh, Luv, HSV/HSL (with an explicit "undefined hue" value), YCbCr, luminance/luma, ΔE76 and ΔE2000, chromatic adaptation (von Kries in LMS, Bradford), optical density and stain matrices, dichromacy simulation matrices, sequential/diverging/qualitative colour maps with their $L^*$ profiles. Spectral tables compiled in as arrays at 5 or 10 nm: CIE 1931 $\bar{x}\bar{y}\bar{z}$, cone fundamentals, D65, Planck's law (computed), a synthetic camera; and the spectral locus for the xy diagram.
- Image representation: planar `Float32Array` channels in **linear light** internally; decode on load, encode only when drawing to the canvas (the canvas is sRGB). A helper pair `loadColour(img) → {r,g,b}` / `drawColour(canvas, {r,g,b}, encoded?)`, in `lib/imageops.ts` next to the grey loaders. Per-channel Gaussian blur and resampling reuse `imageops.ts` (planned there by the watershed and thresholding plans).
- `lib/gl3d.ts` (marching-cubes plan, the shared WebGL renderer): needs a **point-cloud** primitive (per-vertex position and colour, point size, ~50k points), line segments for axes and gamut outlines, and vertex-position interpolation between two buffers for the morph animation (a uniform $t$ in the vertex shader). Context created only while visible, as that plan specifies. Fallback: static figure.
- `lib/linalg.ts` (snakes plan): $3 \times 3$ inverse, solve, condition number (via the symmetric eigen routine on $M^\top M$), cross product, small least squares (3×3 colour correction fit, spectral unmixing), and the shared symmetric eigendecomposition for PCA of colour pixels and the Macenko estimate; null-space basis of a $3 \times n$ matrix (Gram–Schmidt / projection $I - A^\top(AA^\top)^{-1}A$) for the metamer button.
- `lib/histogram.ts` (thresholding plan): add a 2D histogram (two channels, bins, circular axis for hue) and its log-scale drawing; `lib/threshold.ts` (thresholding plan): Otsu on a derived channel, k-means/isodata generalised to 3D, and the Dice/evaluation code for `colourseg`.
- `lib/plot.ts` (morphology plan): spectrum curves, colour bars with $L^*$ profiles, the xy diagram.
- Synthetic images (seeded, `rng(seed)`): coloured objects with shading and noise plus ground truth; spectral chart scenes (smooth random reflectances); Beer–Lambert H&E / H-DAB generator from concentration maps (nuclei/cytoplasm shapes can reuse the "touching nuclei" generator in `imageops.ts`).
- Performance: all per-pixel conversions on 256×256×3 are well under a few ms in TypeScript; the point cloud is a single buffer upload; ΔE2000 over all pixels is the heaviest step and is still fast at this size. No Wasm expected (measure against the ~30 ms rule).
- Tests (node script, `make test`, extending `demos/test/`): sRGB encode∘decode is the identity (and the 0.04045 knee is continuous); $M_{\text{sRGB}}\,(1,1,1)^\top$ = D65 white; the matrix built from primaries + white reproduces the published sRGB matrix; Lab of white = (100, 0, 0) and of greys has $a^* = b^* = 0$; HSV round trip away from grey; ΔE2000 against the published test pairs of Sharma, Wu & Dalal (2005); YCbCr round trip within rounding; the metamer button gives $A(\mathbf{s}_2 - \mathbf{s}_1) \approx 0$ and $\mathbf{s}_2 \ge 0$; grey world equalises channel means; colour deconvolution recovers the concentration maps exactly on a noise-free synthetic image with the true $M$; 3×3 inverse times $M$ = identity.
- Follow `demos/README.md`: fixed seeds, presets + reset, keyboard back to reveal.js (orbit rotation by pointer only), fits 1150×560 on a slide (image 256 px + 3D or histogram panel), labels ≥ 18 px, no hover-only information.

## Open questions

- How long is a "day" (hours, lecture vs exercise)? Unit 4 is dense; if time is short, white balance can move into unit 2 (it is a diagonal matrix in linear RGB) and unit 4 becomes colour deconvolution only.
- Colour deconvolution: core (proposal: it is the strongest link to linear algebra and the most used colour method in bio-image analysis) or optional? The optional unit (colour maps, pseudocolour) is arguably more useful to every student than its "optional" label suggests: could be a short core slot instead of unit 3's k-means part.
- **A real, licensed histology image is wanted** (H&E and ideally H-DAB, brightfield, with known or estimable stain vectors) for `stainsep`, `colourcloud` and `colourseg`. Candidates to check for licence: public CC-BY datasets of H&E tiles, or an image from a collaborating lab with explicit permission. Until then the synthetic Beer–Lambert image carries the demo (it has the advantage of ground truth).
- Also wanted: a colour photograph with a clear licence (shadows, saturated and grey regions, a grey card; ideally the course team's own photo) and a multi-channel fluorescence image for the optional unit. The classic "peppers"/"mandrill" test images have unclear licences.
- Spectral data: CIE 1931 colour matching functions and D65 are standard tables; cone fundamentals (Stockman & Sharpe) are available from the CVRL database: check its terms of use before compiling them into the demos.
- Projector and screen colour: demos assume sRGB, which classroom projectors rarely reproduce (gamut, gamma, white point). Metamer and gamma demos must show the numbers next to the swatches and avoid claims that depend on accurate display; the "squint at 128 vs 188" test may fail on the projector (keep it for the exercise on students' own screens).
- How much of CIELUV, CIEDE2000 and gamut mapping to show? Proposal: one slide each, formulas only for Lab and ΔE76.
- JPEG: chroma subsampling is here; the DCT/quantisation part of JPEG has no home in the current plans (it would fit next to `fourier/`). Leave as a pointer?
- Hyperspectral imaging and fluorescence spectral unmixing: one slide here (proposal), or a unit of their own if the course gets a microscopy-focused day.
- Programming language for the code parts is still open (see README).
