Exercise: Hough transform for lines

Goal

By the end of this exercise you should be able to:

  • turn edge points into sinusoids in (\theta, \rho) and find the line they share,
  • fill a small accumulator by hand and read off the line,
  • predict the effect of gaps, bin size, thresholds, NMS and clutter on the peaks,
  • count the votes and memory of a Hough transform, with and without gradient voting,
  • (math) derive the crossing of two sinusoids and the amplitude–phase form of a point’s curve.

Do the pen-and-paper part first, then the two demos.

Background

Idea What it means
Normal form a line is \{x\cos\theta + y\sin\theta = \rho\}, \theta \in [0°, 180°), \rho signed; origin at the image centre, y up
A point’s curve \rho(\theta) = x\cos\theta + y\sin\theta: a sinusoid; all lines through the point
Accumulator bins (\theta_j, \rho_k); each edge point adds one vote per \theta_j, in the bin of its \rho
Peak a bin with many votes = many collinear points; votes ≈ length of the line in pixels
Threshold, NMS keep bins with ≥ t votes that are the largest in a window around them
Gradient voting vote only for \theta within a small window of the point’s gradient direction (taken modulo 180°)
Segments edge points within ~1 px of a detected line, split at gaps > g, pieces shorter than L_\text{min} dropped

By hand

B1. The points (2, 0), (1, 1), (0, 2). (a) Compute \rho = x\cos\theta + y\sin\theta for each point at \theta = 0°, 45°, 90°, 135° (two decimals). (b) Fill a 4 \times 5 accumulator: rows \theta = 0°, 45°, 90°, 135°, columns \rho = -2, -1, 0, 1, 2 (round each \rho to the nearest integer). Which bin has the most votes? (c) Which line is it? Write it as x\cos\theta + y\sin\theta = \rho and as y = \dots.

B2. Predict, then explain: (a) A straight line of 120 edge pixels gives a peak of 120 votes. The same line is now dashed: 10 pixels on, 10 off. Where is the peak and how high is it? (b) Half of the line’s pixels are moved by one pixel, at random, to either side. What happens to the peak with \Delta\rho = 1, and with \Delta\rho = 3?

B3. Two parallel lines 3 px apart, \rho_1 = 10 and \rho_2 = 13 at the same \theta, each 100 px long. The bins have \Delta\rho = 4 and the NMS window is ±1 bin. (a) Which bins do the two lines vote into if the bins are [8, 12), [12, 16)? And if they are [10, 14)? (b) How many lines are detected in each case? What \Delta\rho and NMS window would you need to see two?

B4. A 512 × 512 image has 10 000 edge points. The accumulator has \Delta\theta = 0.5° and \Delta\rho = 1 px. (a) How many \theta bins and \rho bins are there (the \rho range is ± half the diagonal)? How many bins in total, and how much memory with 4 bytes per bin? (b) How many votes are cast? (c) With gradient voting in a window of ±1° (5 bins), and with 1 bin per point?

B5. The rule of thumb for \Delta\theta: the far end of a line should move by about one pixel. A line through the image centre reaches about half the diagonal away. (a) What \Delta\theta does this give for a 192 × 192 image, and for a 512 × 512 image? (b) A line segment of length 100 px has end points that are uncertain by about 0.5 px. The slide gives its angle uncertainty as \sigma\sqrt2/L. How large is that, in degrees? Is 1° bins a sensible choice for it?

(2, 0) 2.00 1.41 0.00 −1.41
(1, 1) 1.00 1.41 1.00 0.00
(0, 2) 0.00 1.41 2.00 1.41

The bin (45°, 1) has 3 votes; every other bin has at most 1. (c) x\cos 45° + y\sin 45° = \sqrt2 (the bin holds \rho = 1.41), i.e. x + y = 2, or y = 2 - x. The bin centre \rho = 1 is only accurate to half a bin; the exact value comes from the points themselves.

B2. (a) Same position, about half the height: 60 votes. Each present pixel still votes for the same bin; the missing ones simply do not vote. (b) With \Delta\rho = 1, a pixel moved by one changes its \rho by about 1 (times |\cos| of the angle between the move and the normal), so up to half the votes move to the neighbouring bins: the peak drops towards 60 and two or three neighbouring bins rise. With \Delta\rho = 3 most moved pixels stay in the same bin and the peak stays near 120, at the price of a coarser \rho.

B3. (a) Bins [8, 12), [12, 16): the lines vote into neighbouring bins (100 votes each). Bins [10, 14): both lines vote into the same bin, 200 votes. (b) First case: two neighbouring bins with 100 votes each; NMS with a window of ±1 keeps only one of them (the first in scan order at a tie): one detection. Second case: one bin, one detection, with twice the votes. To separate lines 3 px apart, use \Delta\rho \le 1.5 (so they are at least two bins apart) and an NMS window smaller than their distance in bins (for \Delta\rho = 1: window ±1 or ±2).

B4. (a) 180° / 0.5° = 360 angles. The diagonal is 512\sqrt2 = 724 px, so \rho runs over \pm 362: 725 bins. In total 360 \times 725 = 261\,000 bins, about 1.04 MB. (b) 10\,000 \times 360 = 3.6 million votes. (c) 10\,000 \times 5 = 50\,000 votes; with one bin per point 10\,000. The memory is the same; the work drops by a factor 72 or 360.

B5. (a) A rotation by \Delta\theta moves a point at distance d by about d\,\Delta\theta. With $d = $ half the diagonal: 192\sqrt2/2 = 136 px, so \Delta\theta = 1/136 rad = 0.42°; for 512 × 512: 362 px, \Delta\theta = 0.16°. (b) 0.5\sqrt2/100 = 0.0071 rad = 0.41°. Bins of 1° are a bit coarser than the uncertainty, so the votes of the segment fall into one or two angle bins: a robust peak, with an angle known to about half a degree. Much finer bins (0.1°) would split the peak over several bins.

Math questions

B6. (math) Show that the sinusoids of (x_1, y_1) and (x_2, y_2) cross at \tan\theta = (x_2 - x_1)/(y_1 - y_2), and that the line with these parameters passes through both points. Check it for (2, 0) and (0, 2).

B7. (math) Show that x\cos\theta + y\sin\theta = A\cos(\theta - \varphi) with A = \sqrt{x^2 + y^2} and \varphi = \operatorname{atan2}(y, x). What are the amplitude and the position of the maximum of the curve of (1, 1)? Which points have the flattest curves, and why does that make sense?

B8. (math) Show that (\theta + 180°, -\rho) describes the same line as (\theta, \rho). Why does the accumulator only need \theta \in [0°, 180°), and what does that imply for non-maximum suppression near \theta = 0° and \theta = 180°?

Solutions (math)

B6. Equal \rho: (x_1 - x_2)\cos\theta + (y_1 - y_2)\sin\theta = 0, so \tan\theta = -(x_1 - x_2)/(y_1 - y_2) = (x_2 - x_1)/(y_1 - y_2). The equation says (\cos\theta, \sin\theta) \perp \mathbf p_1 - \mathbf p_2, so with \rho = \mathbf n \cdot \mathbf p_1 also \mathbf n \cdot \mathbf p_2 = \mathbf n \cdot \mathbf p_1 + \mathbf n \cdot (\mathbf p_2 - \mathbf p_1) = \rho: both points are on the line. For (2, 0), (0, 2): \tan\theta = (0 - 2)/(0 - 2) = 1, \theta = 45°, \rho = 2\cos 45° = \sqrt2.

B7. Write x = A\cos\varphi, y = A\sin\varphi (polar coordinates of the point). Then x\cos\theta + y\sin\theta = A(\cos\varphi\cos\theta + \sin\varphi\sin\theta) = A\cos(\theta - \varphi). For (1, 1): A = \sqrt2 = 1.41, \varphi = 45°: the maximum is \rho = 1.41 at \theta = 45°. Points near the origin have small amplitude, so flat curves: every line through a point near the origin passes close to the origin, so |\rho| is small for all \theta.

B8. x\cos(\theta + 180°) + y\sin(\theta + 180°) = -(x\cos\theta + y\sin\theta), so the equation for (\theta + 180°, -\rho) is the same equation multiplied by -1: the same set of points. Every line therefore has exactly one representative with \theta \in [0°, 180°). A line with \theta just below 180° is a neighbour of the line with \theta just above 0° and the opposite \rho: the accumulator wraps around with \rho mirrored, and NMS must compare bins across that seam (the demos do), or a nearly vertical line can be detected twice.

Where this leads

A circle has three parameters instead of two; the same voting idea gives the circle Hough transform in the next unit, where the gradient direction saves even more work. For any shape without a formula, the generalised Hough transform stores the shape as a table. And when voting needs too many bins, RANSAC guesses and checks instead.
